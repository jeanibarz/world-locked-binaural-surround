But binaural is computed for a fixed head. Turn your head and the whole scene turns with you, which real rooms never do, so your brain stops believing it and the sound snaps back inside. Movement is the giveaway. Look at a mug on your desk and move your head. It holds its place, because it is really out there. A sound stuck to your head does not. The fix: a webcam tracks your head, and because the app knows where you are facing, it keeps the speakers locked to the room instead. Turn your head now, and only the render changes, the speakers stay put. Does that finally pull the sound out of your head for good? That is the bet I am testing.
